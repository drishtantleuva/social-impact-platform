A tonne of CO₂ avoided says nothing about the community that hosted the project — the jobs created, the clean water delivered, the girls put through school. This platform quantifies that missing half: the social impact of carbon-credit projects, combining project data, UN SDG indicators and AI.

The blind spot

The market optimises for carbon.
Communities are an afterthought.

Two projects can avoid the exact same tonnes of CO₂ — and one transforms a region with jobs, clean cookstoves and schooling while the other does almost nothing for the people living there. Today's carbon price can't tell them apart. That gap distorts where billions of dollars flow.

What social impact looks like

One project, many lives touched

Behind a good carbon project sit concrete human outcomes — each one mapping to a UN Sustainable Development Goal.

The innovation

Three signals, one impact picture

The novelty isn't another carbon model — it's fusing three very different signals into a single, explainable measure of social impact, then letting AI interrogate the project's own claims.

01
Project characteristics

Scale, host-country development level, annual emission reduction, estimated jobs and methodology — the measurable footprint.

02
UN SDG indicators

The 17 Sustainable Development Goals turn vague "co-benefits" into a structured, comparable fingerprint of social value.

03
AI-based analysis

A gradient-boosted model learns the impact pattern; a GPT-4 prompt ladder reads the project narrative and audits its SDG claims with reasoning.

The takeaway

A tonne of carbon is the same everywhere.
Its impact on people is not.

Measuring social impact turns "trust us, it helps the community" into something you can score, compare, explain and fund. That's the contribution of this research — and the start of carbon markets that reward development, not just dilution.
